Lesson 22 · Binary Search Tree, part 1 of 2 · Midterm Q4d

Build a BST from an insertion list

Q4d gives you a list of items and asks you to draw the binary search tree built by inserting them in that order. It's mechanical: one comparison rule, applied at every node, decides where each new item lands.

Retrieval check • before reading on

From Lesson 21: when dequeue(x) is called on an empty queue, what happens to x?

reveal

Nothing. dequeue returns false on its first line and never touches x — x keeps its previous value.

The rule

A BST node holds an item and two child pointers, left and right — same shape as a queue's Node, just two links instead of one, and this course's exam trees are built on int items:

struct BTNode {
    int     item;
    BTNode *left, *right;
    BTNode(int);
};

The very first item becomes the root. For every item after that, start at the root and walk down, comparing at each node:

if (cur->item > newItem)   go LEFT      // new item is SMALLER than this node
else                       go RIGHT     // new item is BIGGER (or equal)

Keep walking that way until you reach a NULL child slot — drop the new node there.

A bouncer at every fork. You arrive at a node holding 40. The bouncer asks: “Is your number smaller than 40?” Smaller → take the left corridor. 40 or bigger → take the right corridor. You meet another bouncer, answer again, and keep going until a corridor has no door yet — that empty doorway is your seat.

You are inserting 15 and you reach a node holding 60. Which way do you go?

Right — 15 is smaller than 60, and cur->item > newItem (60 > 15) means go left.
cur->item > newItem is true here (60 > 15), and that branch goes left. Smaller items go left.
