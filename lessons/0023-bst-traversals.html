Lesson 23 · Binary Search Tree, part 2 of 2 · Midterm Q4e

Read pre/in/post-order from a BST

Q4e asks for the pre-order, in-order and post-order output of a tree. One rule per traversal, applied the same way at every node — no recursion code to write, just the walking pattern.

Retrieval check • before reading on

From Lesson 22: you're inserting a new item and you reach a node where cur->item > newItem is true. Which way do you go?

reveal

Left. The node holds something bigger than the new item, so the new item belongs to its left.

The rule — three orders, one pattern

Each traversal visits every node once. “Visit” means print the item. At every node you do three things — go left, print this node, go right — and the name tells you when the node itself is printed:

TraversalOrder at each nodeNode is printed…
pre-ordernode, left, rightfirst — before either subtree
in-orderleft, node, rightin the middle — between the subtrees
post-orderleft, right, nodelast — after both subtrees

“Left” and “right” here mean the whole left subtree, fully finished, then the whole right subtree — you always dive all the way down the left before you print anything on the right.

Worked example — the tree from Lesson 22

          40
        /    \
      20      50
     /  \    /  \
   10    30 45   60
TraversalOutput
pre-order (node, L, R)40 20 10 30 50 45 60
in-order (L, node, R)10 20 30 40 45 50 60
post-order (L, R, node)10 30 20 45 60 50 40

Walk the in-order one by hand: start at 40, go all the way left to 10 (print 10), back up to 20 (print 20), 20's right is 30 (print 30), back up to 40 (print 40), into the right subtree: left to 45 (print 45), up to 50 (print 50), 50's right 60 (print 60). → 10 20 30 40 45 50 60.

The free check — two parts, do both. In-order output of a BST is (1) the items in ascending order and (2) every node exactly once. 10 20 30 40 45 50 60 is ascending and has all 7.

Checking "is it ascending?" alone is not enough: if you drop a node in the middle, the remaining numbers can still look sorted and you will not notice. So count — a 7-node tree has 7 numbers in every traversal. If the count is short, you skipped a node (usually the root, printed the instant its left subtree finishes).
Critical Exam Trap • Pre vs Post, And Finishing Left First
MistakeFix
Printing a right-subtree node before finishing the left subtreeFully finish the entire left subtree first — every node in it — then the node, then right.
Mixing up pre and postpre = node printed first. post = node printed last. The root is the first number of pre-order and the last number of post-order.
In-order jumps from the left subtree straight into the right, skipping the node between them (the root most often)At every node: left subtree, then print this node, then right subtree. Count the output against the tree — same number of items as nodes.

In which traversal is the root the last item printed?

Yes — post-order prints each node after both its subtrees, so the root comes last.
“post” = node printed after (post) its subtrees. So the root is printed… when?
Produce it • closed book • both skills together

Insert this list into an empty BST, in this order: 50   30   70   20   40   60   80

  1. Draw the tree (boxes or circles, left/right children).
  2. Write its pre-order output.
  3. Write its in-order output — then confirm it is ascending.
  4. Write its post-order output.
reveal answer 
          50
        /    \
      30      70
     /  \    /  \
   20    40 60   80

pre-order (node, L, R): 50 30 20 40 70 60 80

in-order (L, node, R): 20 30 40 50 60 70 80 — ascending ✓

post-order (L, R, node): 20 40 30 60 80 70 50

Checks: 50 is first in pre-order and last in post-order (it is the root). All three outputs contain all seven numbers exactly once.

Produce it again • closed book • the node most often dropped is the root

Insert into an empty BST, in order: 45   20   60   10   30   55   80   25

  1. Draw the tree.
  2. Write pre-order, in-order, post-order.
  3. For the in-order line: count it — 8 nodes, so 8 numbers — and check it is ascending. Both.
reveal answer 
            45
          /    \
        20      60
       /  \    /  \
     10    30 55   80
          /
        25

pre-order: 45 20 10 30 25 60 55 80

in-order: 10 20 25 30 45 55 60 80 — 8 numbers, ascending ✓ (the root 45 sits in the middle, printed the moment the 20 subtree finishes)

post-order: 10 25 30 20 55 80 60 45

Ask your tutor for a larger insertion list to draw if the small one felt easy — the exam tree can have 12–17 nodes, but the rule does not change. Recursion (the code behind these traversals) is a separate September topic for the final.
