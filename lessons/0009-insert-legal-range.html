Lesson 9 of the linked-list crash sequence · Insert (part 1 of 4)

Where can you insert?

The whole job of insert, before any code. Think of the chain as a line of railway carriages, and insert(at, newItem) as squeezing one brand-new carriage into the line at position at. To do that, four things have to happen, always in this order:

  1. Check the request makes sense. Can't insert at a position that doesn't exist yet, or one that's too far past the end.
  2. Build the new carriage. new Node(newItem) on the heap — it exists, but isn't coupled to anything yet.
  3. Walk to the coupling point. Find the carriage that will sit just before the new one (unless the new one is going at the very front — that's a special case, no "before" exists).
  4. Couple it in. Two arrows get rewritten: the new carriage points at what used to come next, and the carriage before it points at the new one instead.

That's the entire shape of insert. This lesson (and the three after it) each take one of those four steps and drill it alone — not because the others don't matter yet, but because trying to hold all four in your head at once is exactly what "insert" feels overwhelming from the outside.

This lesson is step 1 only: is at even a legal position? Nothing about building the node or rewiring arrows yet — that's 0009b/0009c/0010.

Why count+1, not count?

For a list with 3 items, here's every value of at and whether it's legal:

atLegal?Meaning
0Nopositions start at 1, not 0
1Yesnew first item, pushes everything back
2Yesnew second item
3Yesnew third item, pushes the old 3rd back to 4th
4 (= count+1)Yesappend — one slot past the last real item
5Nowould leave a gap — nothing would occupy position 4

count+1 is legal because inserting doesn't require a slot to already exist there — it's creating one. Appending to the end is just "insert one past the last item," so the range of legal positions has to reach one further than the number of items currently there.

A queue of people, and the very back. If 3 people are queuing, you can join at position 1, 2, or 3 (pushing everyone from there back), or you can join at position 4 — the empty spot right behind the last person. You can't join at position 5; there's no one at position 4 yet for you to stand behind.

Contrast with find's guard

find's guard from Lesson 8 was if (position > count) return NULL; — no +1. That's not an inconsistency; it's because the two functions are answering different questions:

FunctionGuardBecause
find(position)position > countcan only point at a node that already exists — max valid position is count
insert(at, ...)at > count+1can create a brand-new slot one past the end — max valid position is count+1

Same-looking guard, different number, for a real reason: find can never return a node that isn't there yet; insert's whole job is to create one.

Quick check — for a list with count = 5, what is the largest legal value of at for insert?

Right — count+1 = 6, meaning "append after the 5th item."
The legal range is 1 through count+1. With count = 5, that's 6.

Second check — one word: what is find's upper bound for position (no +1)?

Right — count, because find only ever points at an existing node, never a new one.
Re-read the contrast table above — find's row names it directly.
